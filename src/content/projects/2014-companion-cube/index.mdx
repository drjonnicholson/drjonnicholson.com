When my wife and I got married in August 2014, we wanted something a little more memorable than a traditional wedding card box and add a bit of our personality into it. As fans of Valve's [Portal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portal_(video_game)) series, we decided to build a full-sized [Companion Cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_Companion_Cube) to collect cards and messages from our guests.

The project started with sketches, measurements, and a lot of reference images from the game. (Apologies for the low quality of the sketch image.)

Over several weekends, the cube took shape from sheet materials, paint, filler, and a healthy amount of patience. The challenge was not only to recreate the look of the Companion Cube, but also to make it practical, durable, and easy to transport to / from the venue.

  <Picture src={functionalImage} formats={['webp','avif']} alt="The Companion Cube taking shape, using pine edging to create the edging and hold the lid in place, with hardboard circles cut out" />
  <Picture src={paintingImage} formats={['webp','avif']} alt="Decorating the Companion Cubein progress, showing the heart stencil" />
  <Picture src={finishedImage} formats={['webp','avif']} alt="Final decorated Companion Cube, including hearts and highlighted lines" />

The finished cube became one of the most recognisable features of our wedding reception. Guests used it to post cards and messages throughout the day, and it proved to be a great conversation starter for gamers and non-gamers alike. We still have our Companion Cube to this day, holding much of our wedding memories.
